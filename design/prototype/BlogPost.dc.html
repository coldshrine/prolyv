People keep asking the same thing under the video: “does it really change that much?” It does — and the point of the diary is to notice it. We brewed a spring Tie Guan Yin in a gaiwan and photographed the liquor after every steep. Below is what we tasted, steep by steep, and the time each one took.

If you have the same tea, or any spring Tie Guan Yin, brew along and attach your session at the bottom. By now 23 people have, and their notes differ in interesting ways.

What we’d change next time

Steep 6 was the turning point: we went to 45 seconds and lost some of the cream. Several people who brewed along kept it at 30 and reported the cream lasting one more steep. We’ll try that in the next video.

Water matters more than leaf here. Two sessions on hard tap water came back flat and slightly bitter from steep 4 — if yours tastes like that, try soft bottled water once before changing anything else.
